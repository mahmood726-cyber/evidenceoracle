## E156 Abstract (CURRENT BODY)

Can machine learning models predict which meta-analytic conclusions are likely to change when new studies are added, enabling proactive evidence surveillance? We trained gradient-boosted classifiers on 42 features extracted from 6,229 Cochrane meta-analyses, labelling each as stable or unstable based on whether the pooled estimate direction or significance changed in the subsequent Cochrane update. EvidenceOracle implements feature extraction from heterogeneity metrics, fragility indices, publication bias indicators, study count, and cumulative Z-statistic trajectories to generate instability risk scores. The model achieved an AUC of 0.925 (95% CI 0.91 to 0.94) on held-out test data, with the top five predictive features being fragility index, cumulative Z-slope, number of studies, I-squared trend, and small-study effect magnitude. Calibration analysis confirmed that predicted probabilities aligned with observed instability rates across deciles within 3 percentage points. Proactive instability prediction could focus living evidence surveillance resources on meta-analyses most likely to change with new evidence. The model was trained on Cochrane reviews and prediction accuracy may differ for non-Cochrane systematic reviews with different methodological standards.
